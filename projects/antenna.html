868 MHz patch antenna

Imperial Rocketry Society, Electronics Team, Oct 2025 to Mar 2026

What it is

The society's first attempt at a conformal antenna, a new idea for the team, for the rocket's telemetry at EuRoC 2026 in Portugal. I wanted to be part of something new.

My role

Team member in a team of three in the electronics sub-team. I did not lead it. We had no prior electronics experience.

What I did

  • Taught myself transmission-line theory, impedance matching and microstrip design from academic literature.
  • Designed an 868 MHz rectangular patch antenna on FR4 mainly by hand calculation (patch dimensions, effective permittivity, edge impedance of about 324 Ω, coaxial probe feed positioned to match 50 Ω), then cross-checked it in openEMS.
  • Laid out the PCB in KiCad. The antenna was manufactured and fitted to the rocket's telemetry system.
  • Derived a U-slot extension to widen the very narrow bandwidth (about 0.06%) and a flexible-substrate route to curve the antenna.

Results

The antenna was manufactured and fitted. The rocket never flew, so there are no measured results.

What went wrong

  • Simulating the U-slot and curved designs needed licensed electromagnetic software (COMSOL, ANSYS). We approached vendors and the university and could not get access, so I concluded that a conformal antenna was not achievable with the tools available.
  • The rocket was permanently grounded and never flew at EuRoC, because its structure was too flexible. About two terms of work were lost, which was disappointing.
